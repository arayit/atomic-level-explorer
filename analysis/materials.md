## What the scan found

- 619901 admissible ladders in 41 species, out of 238 scraped.
- A MEASURED picosecond-window lifetime appears in 31166 of them, in 13 species -- and every one is an ION. No neutral in the whole cache has a level with an ASD lifetime between 20 ps and 1 ns: in neutrals the fast states are the autoionizing resonances above the ionization limit, whose widths are not in a line list at all.
- In 16595 ladders the fast state is a PARKED intermediate rather than the last rung, which is the stronger form of the claim. The rest carry it on the last rung, where it is not a constraint at all -- nothing waits there, so a shorter lifetime is simply a brighter emitter.
- 195534 ladders have a published lifetime for EVERY rung; the other 424367 lean on at least one bound level whose lifetime ASD does not tabulate. Those are not wrong -- almost every bound level below the ionization limit lives far longer than 20 ps -- but they are assumed, not read.
- 0 ladders have a competing direct channel: a single low-order jump from the start straight to the final state on a colour the ladder itself uses. The sequential-only requirement is therefore not a constraint that has to be imposed -- at these total energies no 2- or 3-photon shortcut exists.
- Only 9459 ladders (2%) run on ONE colour; the rest need two or more synchronized bursts. Among the single-colour ones the most productive driver is 2000 nm (2907), 1900 nm (1959), 1600 nm (1652).
- 186257 ladders start from the ground state; the rest need a metastable prepared first, by discharge or optical pumping.
- The search stopped early in 2 species (Fe II, V II) because their level density blows the path budget. Their ladder counts are lower bounds and the particular chains kept are arbitrary -- these are exactly the species where the chance-match column is already saying an energy match proves nothing.

The chance-match column is the check on all of this: it estimates how many levels fall inside one 150 meV hop window by accident. Above about 1, an energy match is not evidence, and the ladder has to stand on its parked lifetimes and hop strengths instead. Fe I sits at 16, Hg II at 0.7.


## Tiers

- **Tier 1** - preparable, closes on driver tuning alone, order 6 or better.
- **Tier 2** - one hard axis: hotter cell, a discharge, or a hop needing chirp/Stark help.
- **Tier 3** - two hard axes, or a ladder that only closes inside the full 150 meV budget.
- **Tier 4** - a ladder exists on paper; preparation or closure makes it a late option.

## What this scan cannot decide

- **Autoionizing widths.** Every neutral candidate ends on a level above the ionization limit. ASD carries no width for those, so the picosecond claim rests on the state class, not on a number. One linewidth measurement per candidate closes this.
- **The strength of the last hop.** Oscillator strengths exist for bound-bound transitions only, so the hop INTO the autoionizing region cannot be scored from a line list; it needs a photoabsorption cross section.
- **Lifetimes quoted as upper bounds.** ASD gives 1/sum(A) over the channels it lists. Parking uses the conservative floor tau * k/n for k of n channels known, but a level with no published A-value at all is invisible to the scan -- which is why Rb I drops out despite its 5s->4d two-photon hop landing 8 meV from 2 x 1030 nm.
- **Anything time-dependent.** Whether the population actually survives the climb is a rate-equation question over the whole burst, not a level-table question.
